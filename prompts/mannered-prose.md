What mannered prose looks like:
- Metaphor substitutes for direct statement: "a dial worth turning" instead of "a parameter worth varying"; "this point earns its keep" instead of "this point still matters".
- Flourish exists to display the writer, not to convey the idea. Readers can tell, and it makes them work harder for no gain.
- Metaphor is imprecise: it drags in connotations the writer did not choose and cannot control.

Rules:
1. For each metaphor or flourish, write the literal statement it stands for.
2. When a literal phrase is available, use it. Say what you mean.
3. Change nothing else: keep technical content, structure, and terminology as they are.
4. If a figure of speech carries meaning no literal phrase covers, keep it and note why.

Output:
1. The edited text.
2. A replacement list, one `before -> after` pair per line. Items kept under rule 4 go last, each with its reason.
